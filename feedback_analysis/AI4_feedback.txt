Technical Audit Report: Phasma AI Options Trading System
Introduction
The Phasma AI Options Trading System, as described in the provided operation document, is a multi-stage pipeline designed to automate the discovery, analysis, simulation, and execution of options trading strategies using AI-powered methods. The system ingests news data, validates companies, performs technical and options analysis, estimates risk and reward via Monte Carlo simulation, applies risk management, and outputs executable trading signals.
Given the prevalence of automation and AI in financial markets, especially in options trading, such a system must meet an exceptionally high bar for reliability, transparency, speed, calibration, and governance. Even small failures in automated decision-making can cause catastrophic financial losses, regulatory violations, or reputational damage.
This report provides a rigorous, end-to-end technical and process audit of each core component of the Phasma system, referencing best practices and industry standards at every stage. Flaws, questionable assumptions, unsafe behaviors, and gaps are identified and explained. Each point is matched with actionable, code-level or architectural recommendations to remediate risk and improve robustness. Concrete implementation suggestions and calibration paths are given where relevant.
1. News Data Source Reliability
Findings
Source Diversity: The document specifies a primary reliance on named news APIs (e.g., Polygon.io, NewsAPI.ai), possibly in combination with real-time sentiment extraction by LLMs. There is little mention of redundancy or fallback mechanisms for missing, delayed, or bad data.
Latency and Coverage: Real-time ingestion is implied, but tolerance of provider outages or region-based news lags is not addressed. For earnings/merger rumors and regulatory headlines, latency of minutes can dramatically affect the quality of any ensuing trading signal.
Sentiment Extraction & Ticker Tagging: The system uses LLMs to extract per-company sentiment from news articles, similar to the capabilities described by Polygon.io's API. However, LLMs can hallucinate, misattribute tickers, or demonstrate drift over news cycles.
Bias and Spam Handling: There's no explicit pipeline step for filtering press releases, clickbait headlines, or low-trust sources. Financial news APIs differ significantly in their filtering, deduplication, and entity extraction quality.
Data Quality Logging: Little evidence exists of robust logging or post-hoc auditability of news ingestion outcomes (e.g., missing articles, extraction errors). Without clear logging, traceability and forensics are impossible.
Risks
Falsified or Manipulated News Feed: Even reputable APIs can include PR stunts and "pump and dump" press releases.
Partial Outage Blind Spots: A failed API or parser can cause the system to be blind to crucial events (e.g., an earnings surprise or SEC enforcement action).
Sentiment Drift and Hallucinations: LLM-driven sentiment scoring can silently degrade, either from model drift or prompt engineering errors, with no "sanity check" fallback.

4. Options Pricing Assumptions
Findings
Base Model: The document references reliance on Black-Scholes, with possibility of implied volatility (IV) surfaces fitted to observed options chains. Assumptions include log-normal price distribution, no early exercise (European options), constant volatility and risk-free rates, and no transaction costs.
Real Market Gaps: Most US equity options are American-style, with discretionary early exercise. Dividends, jumps, volatility clustering, and volatility skew ("smile") are pervasive.
Liquidity, Bid-Ask, and Discrete Price Steps Ignored: The pipeline does not address liquidity or actual fillability at the computed theoretical prices, nor the effect of wide or shifting bid-ask spreads, especially during market events.
Static Volatility: There is no sign of re-fitting or dynamically updating volatility forecasts using the latest implied or realized volatility surfaces, nor fast-response to sudden volatility spikes or regime changes.
Dividend and Corporate Actions: Omission of dividend dates and special situations (splits/spins) introduces major pricing errors.
Risks
Mismodeling American Early Exercise: Underpricing or overpricing options versus real market, producing strategies vulnerable to assignment or arbitrage during market shocks.
Absence of Smiles/Skews: Black-Scholes underestimates move probabilities in tail events; selling "cheap" OTM options using unadjusted models can cause blowups.
Liquidity Blindness: Generating signals to buy/sell options with little or no open interest leads to unfillability or massive slippage.

System Outages Cascade: Unchecked failure in one system can propagate, triggering a cascading outage or avalanche of false signals/orders.
State Loss/Replay: In a system crash, unflushed trades or signals can be lost or double-executed on restart, producing major position or P&L mismatches.
